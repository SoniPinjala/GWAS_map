    How this map was built

    Models and tools were collected from Europe PMC (which indexes PubMed, PMC, bioRxiv and medRxiv) by searching titles for GWAS methods published 2001–2026. Taxonomy revision 3 (26 October 2026) stores statistical architecture, record role, tested unit/input, evidence grade and typed method relationships as separate fields. Corrections were checked against defining abstracts, open full text and official method documentation; records with unresolved architecture are quarantined rather than forced into a cluster.

    Reading the map

    • The overview shows the same catalog at a glance: each large circle is a family, each inner circle an architecture group, and each dot one model. Dot shape shows whether a model needs individual-level genotypes, summary statistics, or accepts either.
    • Groups are defined by what drives the results: the mean model (additive linear or non-linear), how background and relatedness are modelled, whether effects are marginal or joint, the likelihood and null calibration, the tested unit, and whether input is individual-level or summary statistics.
    • Redundant means the same method: a re-implementation (GPU, parallel, cloud) or a newer version, as stated in that tool's own abstract. Different methods in the same group are marked related, because they can still give different results.
    • Differences in hit counts often come from thresholds rather than architecture. The mrMLM family reports at LOD ≥ 3; FarmCPU and BLINK usually use a Bonferroni cutoff. Compare at matched error rates.
    • Single-locus models report every SNP in linkage disequilibrium with a causal variant; joint multi-locus models usually report about one per locus. Fewer hits does not mean less power.
    • Approximate and exact mixed models can disagree at loci of very large effect; if your trait has one, prefer the exact version.
